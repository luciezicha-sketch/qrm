Course Overview

Today's world runs on the accumulation, presentation and interpretation of information at enormous scale. Statistics offers a way to organise that material and to understand the relationships within it. But no statistical model stands alone: each is inextricably tied to a well-considered research question, a logical argument, and hypotheses that can be tested.

This course develops your ability to connect those parts of a research design, to fit a model that enables you to draw reasonable conclusions, and reason through the limitations of any model you build. Our discussions include sampling, measurement, model specification and model fit, drawing examples from political science, economics, medicine and environmental science.

No particular mathematical background is assumed; the emphasis falls on conceptual understanding rather than formal derivation. Work is carried out in R, applied to real research problems.
