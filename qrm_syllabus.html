01

Introduction

Today's world relies much on the accumulation, presentation, and interpretation of large quantities of information. Statistics is a tool that enables us to organize our data in an efficient manner, and provides us with methods that help us understand the relationships that occur in our data and our increasingly complex world. However, any useful statistical model is inextricably linked to well thought-through research question, logical argument, and testable hypotheses. The course focuses on developing students' ability to connect these crucial research design parts to ultimately fit a statistical model and draw valid conclusions. We will also discuss how to reason through the usefulness and the limitation of these models to answer a research question. Our discussions will revolve around the issue of sampling, measurement, model specification, and model fit. We will draw our examples from multiple disciplines, such as political science, economics, medical, and environmental science. The material in this course covers sampling, data display, more advanced issues in linear regression and logistic regression. Students will develop projects, applying their knowledge to real-world problems using elementary computer programming in the R statistical programming package.

02

General Objectives

This course is designed to be accessible for students at all levels of mathematical skill. The focus is put on developing conceptual understanding of statistics without heavy reliance on rigorous mathematical background. In the classical liberal arts sense, the knowledge obtained from this course prepares all students to reason through the usefulness as well as limitations of any quantitative research design. The course aims to start building students' autonomy and independence in answering real world questions using quantitative research. In addition, it provides a solid statistical background for students who wish to continue their statistical education with more advanced courses or those students who plan to perform their own statistical analyses in their coursework and beyond.

03

Learning Outcomes

Upon completion the course aims to provide the students with the following skills:

  1. Understand scientific reasoning and research design, including theory formulation and hypothesis testing.
  2. Critically analyze various types of data and learn to select most appropriate elementary statistical technique to answer research question;
  3. Use statistical programming to enter data, generate descriptive statistics and graphs, and estimate basic statistical models.
  4. Communicate and present statistical results to a variety of audiences

Class Organization

Seminar sessions:

Our Monday sessions are seminar sessions. You are expected to come prepared to class!

Prior to the seminar sessions students are expected to watch a short lecture video (if available for the week), As you watch the videos, make sure to take notes. Highlight if necessary the parts that you did not understand. Additional textbook readings are recommended as a background reading for the statistical concepts introduced in the videos, offering additional details and depth.

If video is not available for the week, students are expected to read the assigned reading. The video can be accessed through the QRM website. In class we will discuss the material in more detail. We will often spend most of the time on practical exercises designed to further deepen the understanding of the weekly topic. There will be minimal to no R work during seminar sessions.

Lab sessions:

Wednesday sessions will be lab sessions. Please, check which group you are in on Brightspace. Lab sessions will be entirely devoted to group replication projects. Students are expected to discuss their progress with the instructor and ask questions about their group projects. Note that our sessions will be intensive and focused on a small group. Thus, you are again expected to come prepared. The details for the group replication project are available in the Replication project handout on the QRM website.

For each group project, students submit one pdf project write-up and Rscript on Brightspace.

Software Requirements

All the statistical work in this class must be done using R statistical software. Any work done in other statistical software will not be accepted and result in a grade deduction. Students will be required to install a copy of R statistical software and R-studio into their computers – both are free and open source. Appendix I contains instructions on how to install R and R-studio.

07

AI Disclosure

Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools may be used in this course under specific guidelines to ensure academic integrity and learning:

Permitted Uses

R Code Development: AI may assist in generating R scripts for statistical analyses. Students must annotate each line of code to explain its function, demonstrating understanding. Use simple, reproducible code to ensure you can explain and replicate it during exams.

Text Editing: AI may be used for editing text (e.g., improving clarity of written assignments), but not for generating substantive content.

Prohibited Uses:

AI must not be used to develop answers for replication assignments (e.g., interpreting results, discussing external validity). All analytical and interpretive work must reflect your own reasoning.

Testing and Accountability: The final exam will test your ability to reproduce and explain R code. Therefore, you are advised to use the replication assignments as an opportunity to practice these skills and use AI as a teaching tool.

Group projects: Replication.

The application and use of statistical techniques cannot be viewed in isolation from the research question and the argument/theory proposed by the researcher. Utilizing statistical models thus depends on the context of the research problem. The group projects will consist of a replication assignment of a published quantitative study. In the lab seminars, students receive a dataset and replication handbook with instructions for each of the assignments. They are required to read the original article in week 1 and be prepared to discuss the article in the lab seminar. There will be a total of two replication assignments throughout the duration of the course and each will be submitted on a bi-weekly basis. Each assignment will focus on different statistical techniques introduced in class. It will not be a full exact replication. Instead students will replicate parts of the study. The aim of the replication assignments is threefold: 1) students learn to study and assess research design of a scientific study; 2) students practice statistical techniques and working with datasets in the context of a real research question; 3) students learn to recognize the consequences of research decisions on the findings.

Each group submits one group assignment report in pdf. Project report must follow the instructions in the Replication Group Project Handbook available on the QRM website. The group project submission must also include a complete and working Rscript showing all statistical work included in the report. Students do not have to submit the datasets unless they have made changes to the dataset. The written report (in pdf) must not contain any Rcode and can only include finished tables and figures. Any research reports that include analyses or figures without a corresponding and functional Rscript will be deemed invalid resulting in a final grade deduction.

Midterm Exam.

In week 5, students will take an in-class midterm exam focused on their understanding of how statistical evidence is evaluated, including the construction of scientific arguments, hypothesis formation, data visualization interpretation, and statistical inference. Students will not use R for this quiz. The exam is designed to take approximately 60 minutes to complete.

Final exam:

In week 8, students will take an in-class final exam that covers their technical statistical knowledge, including interpreting statistical results and understanding the statistical theory underlying the models. Students will be asked to understand basic R code and identify issues with R output. The exam is closed book. Students are permitted to bring a calculator and a formula sheet.

Participation.

The participation grade is based entirely on students' demonstrated preparedness in seminar and lab sessions; that includes primarily the ability to ask relevant questions, answering instructor's questions, and contributing creative and thoughtful insights to in-class discussions. The participation assessment is not based on getting things right each time, but instead trying to engage with the material and demonstrating preparedness in each session.

12

Academic Honesty

LUC's Honour code is stipulated in the LUC Student Handbook. My definition matches LUC's standard. In Quantitative Research Methods, the following rules apply:

  • The group projects are collaborative assignments. Students compile one document per group independently.
  • The midterm and final exam will be in-class and requires that students work alone, books and notes will not be permitted throughout the entire duration of the quiz and exam. Students will be allowed a formula sheet, and a calculator. Copying from another student will be penalized by a failing grade.

Plagiarism includes (but is not limited to) directly copying someone else's work without providing the appropriate citation, or paraphrasing someone else's argument without providing necessary citation. Ignorance regarding the rules of citation or the sources used in your paper is not an excuse and will be treated as any other case of plagiarism. If you have any concerns regarding the citation style and rules, come to see me in my office hours before the paper or assignment is due!
